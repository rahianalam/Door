For most door lock installations, you only need a Phillips screwdriver, a tape measure and a pencil. If the door is not pre-drilled, add a drill, a lock installation kit (2-1/8" hole saw and 1" bit), a sharp 1" chisel and a hammer. Metal doors need bi-metal hole saws and cutting oil. Here is the complete checklist, organized by job, so you buy only what you need.

The Essential Toolkit

Phillips Screwdriver (#2)

Most lock screws are #2 Phillips. A hand screwdriver is best for final tightening because power drivers overtighten easily, which binds latches and strains smart lock motors.

Tape Measure

You will measure backset (2-3/8" or 2-3/4"), door thickness (1-3/8" or 1-3/4") and hole diameters. Measure before you buy.

Pencil and Marking Knife

A pencil marks hole centers; a utility or marking knife scores the outline of latch plates for crisp chisel cuts.

Tools for Drilling a New Lock

Drill/Driver

A cordless 18V or 20V drill handles wood doors easily. For steel doors, a corded drill with variable speed gives better control at low RPM.

Lock Installation (Boring) Kit

The single most useful purchase. It includes a 2-1/8" hole saw for the face hole, a 1" spade or Forstner bit for the edge hole and usually a clamp-on jig that keeps both holes square and centered. Kits cost about $15 to $40. See how to drill a door for a lock for the technique.

Wood Chisel (1") and Hammer

Used to mortise latch and strike plates so they sit flush. Keep the chisel sharp: a dull chisel tears wood instead of slicing it.

Combination Square

Carries layout lines around the door edge so the face and edge holes line up.

Latch Mortising Template (Optional)

A plunge-style template cuts a perfect latch recess with a hammer tap. Handy if you install many locks.

Tools for Metal and Fiberglass Doors

  • Bi-metal hole saw (2-1/8"): cuts steel skins cleanly; wood hole saws dull instantly.
  • Cutting oil: keeps the saw cool and the cut clean.
  • Center punch: stops the pilot bit wandering on smooth steel.
  • Metal file or deburring tool: removes sharp edges inside the hole.
  • Step bit: useful for edge holes in steel.

More detail: how to install a lock on a metal door.

Tools for Mortise Locks

  • Spade or Forstner bits matched to the lock body width (often 3/4" to 1")
  • 1/2" and 1" chisels
  • A mortising jig (rentable) to keep the pocket square
  • Marking gauge and square

Full process: how to install a mortise lock.

Materials Worth Having on Hand

  • 3" wood screws for strike plates and the top hinge. They anchor into the stud and resist kick-ins.
  • Heavy-duty strike plate or strike box for exterior doors.
  • Lipstick or chalk to mark exactly where the bolt meets the jamb.
  • Wood filler and touch-up paint for old holes when changing hardware.
  • Fresh batteries for smart and keypad locks.
  • Graphite or dry PTFE lubricant for cylinders. Avoid oil-based sprays that attract dirt.

Safety Gear

  • Safety glasses for drilling and chiseling
  • Work gloves for metal doors and sharp burrs
  • Dust mask when drilling fiberglass or MDF

Frequently Asked Questions

What tools do I need to install a deadbolt?

On a pre-drilled door, a screwdriver. On a new door, a drill, lock installation kit, chisel, hammer, square and tape measure. Step-by-step: how to install a deadbolt.

Can I use a regular drill bit instead of a hole saw?

No. The 2-1/8" face hole needs a hole saw. Smaller bits will not cut a clean, accurately sized hole.

Do I need a chisel to install a door lock?

Only if the latch or strike has a rectangular plate that must be recessed. Drive-in latches need no chisel.

Should I use a power drill to tighten lock screws?

Use it to drive long strike screws, but finish lock screws by hand to avoid overtightening.

Where can I get a lock installation kit?

Home improvement stores and online retailers sell them for about $15 to $40.

The Bottom Line

A screwdriver and tape measure cover most lock swaps. Add a lock installation kit, chisel and drill when you need new holes, and bi-metal tools for steel doors. Do not forget 3" screws for the strike plate. They cost a few dollars and make the biggest difference to security.
