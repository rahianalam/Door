In 2026, professional door lock installation typically costs $75 to $200 per lock in the US, plus the lock itself, which ranges from about $20 for a basic knob to $300 or more for a smart deadbolt. Installing a lock yourself on a pre-drilled door costs only the hardware and about 15 to 60 minutes. Prices rise for new holes, mortise locks, commercial doors, rekeying and after-hours calls. What Affects the Price

1. Whether the Door Is Pre-Drilled

Swapping a lock into existing holes is the cheapest job. Drilling new holes for a deadbolt adds time and skill. Expect $25 to $75 more.

2. Lock Type and Complexity

Mortise locks require cutting a deep pocket in the door, so labor costs can double. Smart locks need app setup and sometimes hub or Wi-Fi configuration.

3. Door Material

Steel and fiberglass doors need bi-metal hole saws and extra care; glass and aluminum storefront doors need specialty hardware. See installing a lock on a metal door.

4. Rekeying and Keying Alike

Making new locks use one key typically costs $15 to $40 per cylinder at the time of installation. Master key systems for offices cost more.

5. Strike Reinforcement

Upgrading to a heavy-duty strike or a door reinforcement kit adds $20 to $100 in parts. It is the most cost-effective security upgrade you can buy.

6. Trip Fees, Timing and Location

Many locksmiths charge a service call fee ($50 to $100), often waived or included when installing. After-hours, weekend and emergency rates can be 1.5 to 2 times higher. Urban areas usually cost more.

Example Project Costs

  • Replace a front door knob and deadbolt (existing holes, combo pack): about $60 to $150 in hardware; $100 to $200 installed by a pro.
  • Add a deadbolt to a door that has none: about $25 to $120 hardware; $100 to $200 labor.
  • Install a smart deadbolt: about $150 to $350 hardware; $100 to $200 labor including setup.
  • Change all interior door handles in a 3-bedroom home (10 doors): about $200 to $600 in hardware; $400 to $900 labor, or a weekend of DIY.

How to Save Money

  1. Do simple swaps yourself. A knob, lever or deadbolt into existing holes is a beginner job. Start with our deadbolt guide.
  2. Buy keyed-alike packs. You avoid separate rekeying fees.
  3. Rekey instead of replacing. If the locks work, rekeying costs less than new hardware. See changing a lock without changing the knob.
  4. Bundle jobs. One visit for several locks usually costs less per lock than separate calls.
  5. Get written quotes. Ask for the total including trip fee and parts, and avoid callers who quote very low then add fees on site.

When It Is Worth Paying a Professional

  • Mortise locks, commercial doors and fire-rated doors
  • Master key systems and access control
  • High-security locks with restricted keys
  • Doors that are out of square, damaged or need frame repair
  • Any job where you are not confident drilling a costly exterior door

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a locksmith charge to install a lock?

Typically $75 to $200 per lock in 2026, depending on lock type, whether holes exist, and your location, plus the cost of the lock.

How much does it cost to install a deadbolt?

About $75 to $150 in labor if the holes exist and $100 to $200 if new holes are drilled, plus $25 to $120 for the deadbolt.

Is it cheaper to rekey or replace locks?

Rekeying is usually cheaper, about $15 to $40 per cylinder plus a service fee, as long as the existing locks are in good condition.

How much does smart lock installation cost?

About $100 to $200 for professional installation and setup, plus $150 to $350 for the lock.

Can I install a door lock myself?

Yes. Most residential locks install with a screwdriver in under an hour on a pre-drilled door.

The Bottom Line

Budget roughly $75 to $200 per lock for professional installation plus hardware, more for drilling, mortise or commercial work. For standard knobs, levers, deadbolts and keypad locks on pre-drilled doors, DIY saves the labor entirely. Spend the savings on a better lock and a reinforced strike.
