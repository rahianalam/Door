To install a lock on double doors, you lock the active door to the inactive door: install a knob or lever and deadbolt on the active door, and secure the inactive door top and bottom with flush bolts or surface bolts so it cannot move. Without bolts on the inactive door, even a strong deadbolt can be forced because both doors swing together. This guide shows you how to do both parts on wooden French or entry double doors.

Quick Answer: The Two Parts of a Double Door Lock

Why Double Doors Need Extra Hardware

On a single door, the deadbolt shoots into a solid frame. On double doors, the active door's bolt shoots into the edge of the other door. If that inactive door is only held by a weak catch, a kick pushes both doors open together. Securing the inactive door into the head frame and the floor turns it into a solid "frame" for the active door's lock.

Choose Your Inactive Door Bolts

  • Manual flush bolts: recessed into the door edge, top and bottom, operated by a small lever. Clean look; the standard choice for French and entry doors.
  • Automatic flush bolts: engage automatically when the active door closes. Used on commercial pairs and fire-rated doors.
  • Surface bolts: mounted on the door face. Easier to install, more visible. Good for interior pairs, closets and sheds.
  • Multipoint locks: common on uPVC and some European doors; one handle drives several bolts. Usually supplied with the door.

Tools and Materials

  • Knob/lever and deadbolt set for the active door (Grade 2 or better for exterior)
  • Pair of flush bolts (top bolt often 12" long, bottom 6") and their strikes
  • Dummy handle for the inactive door
  • Drill with 1/2" or 5/8" bits and a chisel
  • Lock installation kit (2-1/8" hole saw, 1" bit) if the active door is not drilled
  • Tape measure, square, screwdriver, 3" screws for strikes

Step-by-Step: Installing Locks on Double Doors

Step 1: Decide Which Door Is Active

Pick the door you naturally reach for. On outswing patio pairs, check which way the astragal (the vertical strip covering the gap) overlaps: the inactive door usually carries it.

Step 2: Install Flush Bolts on the Inactive Door

Mark the bolt locations on the door edge, near the top and bottom. Drill a hole for the rod into the door edge to the depth specified, then mortise the bolt faceplate flush with a chisel. Insert each bolt and screw it in. Test that the levers slide freely.

Step 3: Drill the Head and Floor Strikes

Close the inactive door and extend the bolts to mark the head jamb and threshold or floor. Drill holes for the bolts (usually 1/2" to 5/8") about 3/4" deep, and fit the strike or dust-proof floor strike so dirt does not block the bottom bolt.

Step 4: Install the Active Door Lock and Deadbolt

Fit the knob or lever and the deadbolt as you would on a single door. If the active door is not pre-drilled, follow our deadbolt installation guide and front door lock installation guide.

Step 5: Install Strikes on the Inactive Door Edge

Close both doors and mark where the latch and deadbolt hit the inactive door edge. Bore a 1" hole about 1-1/4" deep for the deadbolt and a shallower one for the latch, then mortise both strike plates flush.

Step 6: Fit the Dummy Handle

Mount a matching dummy handle on the inactive door so both doors look the same.

Step 7: Test the Whole System

Lock the inactive door with its flush bolts, close the active door and lock the deadbolt. Push firmly on both doors at the center: there should be no movement. Check the gap between the doors as well. Ideally it is about 1/8"; see what is the gap between double doors.

Common Mistakes

  • Relying on ball or roller catches. They hold a door shut, but offer no security.
  • Skipping the top bolt. Both bolts are needed to stop the inactive door twisting.
  • Shallow floor holes. The bottom bolt must fully engage.
  • A large gap between the doors. It weakens the lock and lets the bolt be pried. Adjust hinges first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you secure the inactive door of a double door?

Install flush bolts at the top and bottom of its edge that shoot into the head frame and the floor or threshold.

Can you put a deadbolt on double doors?

Yes. Install it on the active door so it shoots into a strike on the inactive door, and secure the inactive door with flush bolts.

What lock is best for French doors?

A keyed handle and deadbolt on the active door plus flush bolts on the inactive door, or a multipoint lock if the doors were built for one.

Do I need an astragal?

On exterior pairs, yes. It covers the gap between the doors, blocks drafts and prevents prying at the latch.

Can I install double door locks myself?

Yes, with a drill, chisel and some patience. Allow 1.5 to 3 hours for both doors.

The Bottom Line

A double door is only as strong as its inactive leaf. Bolt that door firmly into the head and floor, then install a quality lock and deadbolt on the active door with strikes mortised into the inactive edge. The result locks as solidly as a single door.
