To drill a door for a lock, mark the center using the lock's paper template at the correct backset (2-3/8" or 2-3/4"), drill a 2-1/8" hole through the door face with a hole saw from both sides, then drill a 1" hole into the door edge until it meets the face hole. A clamp-on drilling jig keeps everything square. The whole job takes 20 to 30 minutes on a wood door. Below are the exact measurements, the technique, and how to fix mistakes.

Standard Door Lock Hole Measurements

MeasurementStandard SizeNotes
Face (cross) bore2-1/8" diameterFor knobs, levers and deadbolts
Edge (latch) bore1" diameterFor the latch or bolt
Backset2-3/8" (common interior) or 2-3/4" (common exterior)Door edge to center of face bore
Latch plate mortiseAbout 1" x 2-1/4", 1/8" deepOnly for faceplate (rectangular) latches
Knob/lever heightAbout 36" to 38" from the floorMatch other doors in the home
Deadbolt heightAbout 5-1/2" to 6" above the knobCenter to center

Some older and specialty doors use a 1-1/2" face bore or a 5" backset for large decorative hardware. Always check the template supplied with your lock.

What Is Backset and How Do You Choose It?

Backset is the distance from the edge of the door to the center of the face hole. Most modern latches adjust between 2-3/8" and 2-3/4". Choose:

  • 2-3/8" for most interior doors and narrow stiles.
  • 2-3/4" for most exterior doors, which have wider stiles, so the handle sits farther from the frame and your knuckles do not hit the jamb.

If you are adding a deadbolt above an existing knob, match the knob's backset.

How to Drill a Door for a Lock: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Mark the Height

Measure up from the floor (about 36" to 38" for a knob) and draw a short level line across the door edge. On paneled doors, keep the hole within the solid stile and away from joints.

Step 2: Position the Template

Fold the paper template along the line marked for your door thickness and tape it to the door, with the fold on the door edge. Mark the center of the face hole for your backset and the center of the edge hole. If you are using a jig, clamp it on at the line instead.

Step 3: Drill the 2-1/8" Face Hole

Put the hole saw's pilot bit on your mark and hold the drill level and square to the door. Drill at moderate speed until the pilot bit just pokes through the far side. Stop.

Step 4: Finish From the Other Side

Move to the other face, put the pilot bit in the small exit hole and finish the cut. This prevents the veneer or paint from splintering, which would show around the lock.

Step 5: Drill the 1" Edge Hole

Center the 1" bit on the edge mark, hold it square in both directions, and drill until you break into the face hole. A jig makes this easy. Keep the drill level or the latch will bind.

Step 6: Mortise the Latch Plate (If Needed)

Insert the latch, hold the faceplate square and score around it with a utility knife. Remove the latch and chisel a recess about 1/8" deep so the plate sits flush. Drive-in latches with a round collar just tap in with a block of wood.

Step 7: Test-Fit the Lock

Install the latch and handles. Turn them several times. Everything should move freely with no rubbing.

Step 8: Drill the Strike in the Frame

Close the door, mark where the latch meets the jamb, drill a 1" hole about 1/2" deep for a latch (1-1/4" deep for a deadbolt), and mortise the strike plate flush. See how to install a strike plate.

Drilling Different Door Types

  • Solid wood or solid core: the easiest. Standard hole saw and spade bit.
  • Hollow core: drill only at the factory lock block, usually marked on the edge or found about 36" up on the handle side.
  • Fiberglass: use a sharp bi-metal saw at moderate speed; wear a dust mask.
  • Steel: center punch, bi-metal saw, cutting oil, low speed, from both sides. Guide: installing a lock on a metal door.

How to Fix Common Drilling Mistakes

MistakeFix
Splintered face around the holeChoose a lock with a larger rose, or fill and paint
Edge hole off-centerEnlarge slightly with a file so the latch sits straight; or use a latch with a larger faceplate
Wrong backset drilledUse an adjustable latch; if far off, fill with a dowel plug and redrill
Hole drilled in the wrong placeGlue in a wood plug or use a door reinforcer/cover plate, then redrill
Latch recess too deepShim behind the faceplate with thin cardboard or veneer

Frequently Asked Questions

What size hole do I drill for a door knob?

A 2-1/8" hole through the face and a 1" hole through the edge.

What is the standard backset for a door?

2-3/8" is common on interior doors and 2-3/4" on exterior doors.

Can I drill a door lock hole without a kit?

You can use a separate 2-1/8" hole saw and 1" spade bit, but a kit with a jig makes it much more accurate.

How high should a door lock be?

Knobs and levers are usually 36" to 38" from the floor, with a deadbolt about 6" above.

How do I keep the door from splintering?

Drill the face hole from both sides, using sharp bits and moderate speed.

The Bottom Line

Drilling a door for a lock comes down to three numbers (2-1/8" face hole, 1" edge hole, correct backset) and one technique: drill from both sides, square and level. Use the paper template or a jig, test-fit before finishing, and the lock will turn smoothly for years. Next step: install your deadbolt.
