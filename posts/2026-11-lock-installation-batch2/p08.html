Office door lock installation depends on two things: the door (wood, hollow metal or aluminum glass storefront) and the lock function you need (office, storeroom, classroom or privacy). For a typical wood or hollow metal office door that is already prepped, installing a Grade 2 cylindrical lever with an "office" function takes 20 to 30 minutes. Commercial fire-rated doors and access control systems have extra rules, so this guide explains what you can do yourself and when to call a commercial locksmith.

Lock Functions Explained

Commercial levers are sold by "function," a standard ANSI/BHMA description of how they lock. The most common for offices:

  • Office/Entry: push or turn button inside locks the outside lever; key unlocks. The inside lever always opens the door.
  • Storeroom: outside lever is always locked; key retracts the latch. Inside lever always free. Good for supplies and IT rooms.
  • Classroom: outside lever is locked or unlocked with a key from the outside. Inside always free.
  • Classroom security / intruder: can be locked from inside with a key, so staff do not have to step into a hallway.
  • Privacy: button lock with emergency release, for restrooms and wellness rooms.
  • Passage: no lock.

In almost all commercial functions the inside lever always opens the door. This is a life-safety requirement for egress, so avoid double-keyed locks on office doors.

Grades: How Tough Should an Office Lock Be?

  • Grade 1: 1,000,000 cycles. Main entries and heavy-traffic doors.
  • Grade 2: 400,000 cycles. Most private offices and storerooms.
  • Grade 3: residential duty. Not recommended for commercial doors.

Identify Your Door Prep

  • Cylindrical (bored) prep: a 2-1/8" face hole and 1" edge hole, like residential doors, often with a 2-3/4" backset. Most office levers fit this.
  • Mortise prep: a deep rectangular pocket in the edge. Needs a mortise lock of matching size. See how to install a mortise lock.
  • Narrow stile: aluminum glass doors with slim frames. Need narrow-backset locks (often 31/32" or 1-1/8").
  • Hollow metal: steel doors usually come prepped for a specific lock. See installing a lock on a metal door.

How to Install a Cylindrical Office Lever

Tools: screwdriver, tape measure, the lock's spanner tool (often included). Time: 20 to 30 minutes.

Step 1: Confirm Handing and Function

Check the box for function and whether the lever is reversible. Stand on the outside (key side) of the door: hinges on the left means a left-hand door.

Step 2: Remove the Old Lock

Commercial levers are usually removed by pressing a small release with a pin or tool through a slot in the inside lever, then unscrewing the inside rose with a spanner. Remove the chassis and latch.

Step 3: Install the Latch

Insert the latch with the bevel facing the strike, then screw the faceplate in. On commercial latches, the small "deadlatch" plunger must face the strike side so it holds the latch against slipping.

Step 4: Install the Chassis

Slide the lock chassis in from the outside so it engages the latch. Adjust it for door thickness if required (often by rotating the rose liner).

Step 5: Attach the Inside Rose and Lever

Fit the inside mounting plate and rose, tighten with the spanner, then push the inside lever on until it clicks.

Step 6: Install the Strike and Test

Make sure the latch catches and the deadlatch plunger stops against the strike lip without entering the opening. Test the lock function with the key and button, and confirm the inside lever always opens the door.

Keying and Master Key Systems

Offices often need several keys with different access levels: a key for each office, a master key for managers and a grand master for facilities. This is a master key system, set up by a locksmith. Interchangeable core (IC) cylinders make rekeying fast when staff change. If you are adding locks to an existing building, match the keyway so the new lock joins the system.

Keypad and Access Control Locks

Standalone keypad levers install like a standard lever and are programmed at the door. Networked access control (cards, fobs or phones) adds software, user management and audit trails, and usually requires wiring or a gateway. For multi-tenant buildings, coordinate with building management. For simpler keypads, see how to install a keypad door lock.

Code and Safety Rules for Office Doors

  • Egress: doors on an exit path must open from the inside without a key, special knowledge or effort.
  • ADA: use lever handles, not knobs, mounted between 34" and 48" above the floor.
  • Fire-rated doors: use only listed hardware and do not cut new preps in the field without the manufacturer's approval, or the fire label can be voided.
  • Landlord rules: in leased space, check your lease before changing locks.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best lock for an office door?

A Grade 2 or Grade 1 cylindrical lever with an office/entry function suits most private offices. Use storeroom function for supply rooms.

Can I install an office door lock myself?

On a prepped wood or hollow metal door, yes. For fire-rated doors, master key systems and access control, use a commercial locksmith.

What is the difference between office and storeroom function?

Office function can be left unlocked; storeroom function is always locked from outside and needs a key every time.

Do office doors need lever handles?

In most commercial buildings, yes, to meet ADA accessibility requirements.

How much does it cost to install an office lock?

Grade 2 commercial levers typically cost $80 to $250. Locksmith installation runs around $75 to $200 per door, more for master keying or access control.

The Bottom Line

Pick the right function first (office, storeroom or privacy), choose Grade 2 or better, and match the door prep. On a prepped door, a cylindrical office lever is a quick install. Keep egress and fire-door rules front of mind, and bring in a commercial locksmith for master keying and access control.
