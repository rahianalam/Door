To replace a door lock cylinder, remove the lock's interior screws or retaining clip, pull the old cylinder out, and fit an identical-type cylinder (same brand keyway, same style, and the right length for your door). Deadbolt and knob cylinders swap in 10 to 20 minutes with a screwdriver; mortise and euro cylinders are held by a single set screw or retaining screw. Replacing just the cylinder is cheaper than replacing the whole lock when the hardware still works but you need new keys.

Replace the Cylinder, Rekey, or Replace the Lock?

  • Rekey: change the pins so the old cylinder takes a new key. Cheapest if a locksmith or a rekeyable lock (like Kwikset SmartKey) is available.
  • Replace the cylinder: best for euro and mortise cylinders, worn or damaged cylinders, or upgrading to a high-security cylinder.
  • Replace the whole lock: when the bolt or handle mechanism is worn, or you want a new style. See changing a lock without changing the knob.

How to Replace a Deadbolt Cylinder

  1. Unlock and open the door.
  2. Remove the interior thumb turn by unscrewing the two through-bolts.
  3. Pull the exterior cylinder out from the outside.
  4. Check the replacement: same brand or compatible model, same tailpiece type. Many deadbolt cylinders are sold only with the matching interior piece, so you may replace both sides.
  5. Fit the new cylinder with the keyway facing down, pass the tailpiece through the bolt, attach the thumb turn and tighten evenly.
  6. Test with the door open, then closed.

How to Replace a Knob or Lever Cylinder

  1. Remove the knob or lever from the door (see the manufacturer instructions; many use a release slot on the neck).
  2. On the removed exterior knob, insert the key, turn it slightly, and release the cylinder retaining clip or ring, often with a small screwdriver or a special tool.
  3. Pull the cylinder out of the knob.
  4. Insert the new cylinder of the same make, reinstall the clip and refit the knob.

Because knob cylinders vary by brand, it is often simpler to rekey or replace the whole knob.

How to Replace a Euro Cylinder (uPVC and Composite Doors)

Step 1: Measure the Cylinder

Measure from the center of the retaining screw hole to each end of the cylinder, for example 35/45 mm. The cylinder should protrude no more than about 3 mm past the handle plate on the outside. A cylinder that sticks out further is easier to snap.

Step 2: Remove the Retaining Screw

Open the door and remove the long screw in the edge plate, directly in line with the cylinder.

Step 3: Rotate the Cam and Withdraw

Insert the key and turn it about 10 to 15 degrees either way until the cam lines up with the cylinder body. The cylinder will then slide out.

Step 4: Fit the New Cylinder

With the key inserted and the cam aligned, slide the new cylinder in, refit the retaining screw and tighten.

Step 5: Test

Lock and unlock several times with the door open, then closed. If the door double-locks with the handle lifted, test that too. See how to double lock a door for multipoint locks.

Security tip: choose an anti-snap, anti-bump, anti-pick cylinder rated to recognized standards (in the UK, look for TS 007 3-star or SS312 Diamond).

How to Replace a Mortise Cylinder

  1. Open the door and find the cylinder set screw on the lock faceplate edge, usually under the faceplate trim.
  2. Loosen the set screw a few turns.
  3. Unscrew the cylinder counterclockwise from the outside.
  4. Screw in the new cylinder of the same length and cam type until the keyway is straight and level with the cam in the correct position.
  5. Tighten the set screw and test that the key operates the bolt. Adjust by one turn if it does not.

Choosing the Right Replacement

  • Brand and keyway: must match the lock body (for example, Schlage C, Kwikset KW1).
  • Length: critical for euro and mortise cylinders.
  • Cam or tailpiece type: must match the lock mechanism.
  • Finish: match the rest of the hardware.
  • Keyed alike: buy cylinders keyed alike if you are replacing several.

Common Mistakes

  • Wrong euro cylinder size: too long is a security risk; too short will not engage.
  • Forgetting to align the cam: the cylinder will not slide out or in.
  • Overtightening the retaining or through-bolts: causes a stiff key.
  • Mixing brands: a cylinder from another brand rarely fits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I replace just the lock cylinder?

Yes. Euro, mortise and rim cylinders are designed to be swapped. US deadbolt cylinders are usually replaced with the matching interior piece.

How do I know what size euro cylinder I need?

Measure from the center of the retaining screw to each end, and choose a size that protrudes no more than about 3 mm outside.

Is it cheaper to rekey or replace a cylinder?

Rekeying is usually cheaper. Replacing is better when the cylinder is worn or you want higher security.

Why won't my euro cylinder come out?

The cam is not aligned. Insert the key and turn it slightly until the cam sits inside the cylinder profile.

Do I need a locksmith to replace a cylinder?

No for most cylinders. A locksmith helps with high-security, master keyed or interchangeable-core systems.

The Bottom Line

Identify your cylinder type, measure carefully, and buy a matching replacement. Deadbolt, euro and mortise cylinders each swap in minutes with a screwdriver once you know the trick for that type. For a new lock instead, start with our complete door lock installation guide.
