To install a strike plate so the latch catches every time, mark exactly where the latch meets the door jamb (lipstick or chalk on the latch works well), drill a 1" hole at that point, chisel a shallow recess so the plate sits flush, and fasten it with screws. For deadbolts and exterior doors, use 3" screws that reach the wall stud. A misaligned strike is the most common reason a door will not latch or a deadbolt will not lock, so precise placement matters more than anything else.

Types of Strike Plates

  • Standard strike: a flat plate with a curved lip that guides the latch in. Supplied with most knobs and levers.
  • Full-lip strike: a longer lip for doors with thick trim or wide gaps.
  • Box strike: includes a metal cup that lines the hole. Cleaner and stronger.
  • Heavy-duty security strike: long plate with four or more screw holes, designed for 3" screws. The best upgrade for exterior doors.
  • Adjustable strike: lets you fine-tune the latch position, useful on doors that seasonally move.

Tools You Need

  • Drill with 1" spade or Forstner bit
  • 1" chisel and hammer
  • Utility knife and pencil
  • Lipstick, chalk or a dry-erase marker
  • Screwdriver and 3" screws

How to Install a Strike Plate: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Mark the Latch Position Precisely

Rub lipstick or chalk on the end of the latch bolt (or deadbolt). Close the door gently against the jamb and, for a deadbolt, turn the key or thumb turn so the bolt touches the jamb. The mark shows the exact center. This is more accurate than measuring.

Step 2: Drill the Hole

Drill a 1" hole on the mark: about 1/2" to 5/8" deep for a latch, at least 1-1/4" deep for a deadbolt. Keep the drill level and square to the jamb.

Step 3: Trace the Plate

Center the strike plate over the hole and trace its outline with a pencil, then score the outline with a utility knife to prevent splitting.

Step 4: Chisel the Recess

Chisel inside the outline to the thickness of the plate so it sits flush with the jamb. Too shallow and the door rubs; too deep and the latch catches on the edge.

Step 5: Fasten the Plate

Drill pilot holes and fasten the plate. For exterior doors and deadbolts, replace the short screws with 3" screws angled slightly toward the center of the wall so they bite into the stud behind the jamb.

Step 6: Test

Close the door with gentle pressure. The latch should click in without you pushing, and the door should not rattle. A deadbolt should extend fully without friction.

Quick fix for small misalignments: enlarge the opening in the strike plate by up to 1/8" with a metal file instead of moving the whole plate.

Why 3" Screws Matter

Most door kick-ins succeed because the strike plate is held only by short screws in the thin door jamb. A 3" screw passes through the jamb and the gap, then anchors into the solid framing. Combined with a heavy-duty strike, this greatly increases resistance to forced entry for a few dollars. Do the same on the top hinge for extra strength.

Moving a Strike Plate

If you need to move the strike more than about 1/8", fill the old screw holes and recess with glued wood plugs or epoxy filler, let it cure, sand flush, then cut the new recess. Never leave screws in half-old, half-new holes. They will strip.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I line up a strike plate with the latch?

Mark the end of the latch with lipstick or chalk, close the door so it touches the jamb, and center the plate on the mark.

How deep should the hole for a deadbolt strike be?

At least 1-1/4", so the 1" bolt can fully extend.

Why won't my door latch?

Usually the strike is misaligned or the door has sagged. Tighten the hinges and adjust or file the strike.

Do I need to chisel for a strike plate?

Yes, so the plate sits flush with the jamb. A raised plate causes rubbing.

What screws should I use for a strike plate?

Use 3" screws on exterior doors and deadbolts so they anchor into the wall stud.

The Bottom Line

Precise placement makes a door latch every time: mark the latch position directly, drill to the right depth, recess the plate flush, and fasten with 3" screws on exterior doors.
