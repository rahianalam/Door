Most locksmiths will install a lock you bought yourself, but many charge a slightly higher labor rate, and they usually will not warranty the hardware, only their workmanship. Some decline customer-supplied locks entirely, especially very cheap or unbranded ones they consider insecure or unreliable. Call ahead, tell them the exact brand and model, and ask for a full quote including the trip fee.

What might they refuse?Unbranded locks, damaged boxes, missing parts, or locks unsuited to the door

Why Some Locksmiths Charge More or Say No

  • Lost hardware margin: part of a locksmith's income comes from selling locks. Without that, some adjust their labor price.
  • Quality concerns: cheap locks can fail soon after installation, and the customer may blame the installer.
  • Wrong parts: a lock with the wrong backset, door thickness range or function means a wasted trip.
  • Warranty clarity: they cannot guarantee hardware they did not supply.

How to Make the Job Go Smoothly

  1. Measure first. Check backset (2-3/8" or 2-3/4"), door thickness and hole size before buying.
  2. Buy a recognized brand, with an ANSI/BHMA grade for exterior doors (Grade 2 or better).
  3. Keep everything in the box: keys, screws, strike plates, adapters and instructions.
  4. Tell the locksmith the brand and model when you call, and send a photo of the door edge if asked.
  5. Ask for a written quote that includes the trip fee, labor, any drilling and rekeying.
  6. Ask about keying alike so one key opens all your doors.
  7. Confirm the workmanship guarantee.

Smart Locks You Bought Online

Locksmiths increasingly install smart and keypad locks. Ask whether they will also set up the app and Wi-Fi, which some charge extra for. Make sure your phone and Wi-Fi password are available during the visit. Many smart locks are straightforward to fit yourself: see how to install a keypad door lock and installing a smart lock without drilling.

Should You Just Install It Yourself?

If the door is already drilled and you have a standard knob, lever, deadbolt or keypad lock, installation usually takes 15 to 45 minutes with a screwdriver. Consider a locksmith when the job involves:

  • Drilling a new deadbolt hole in an expensive exterior door
  • Mortise locks or commercial hardware (see mortise lock installation)
  • Rekeying or master keying
  • Doors that are out of square or damaged

Red Flags When Hiring

  • Very low phone quotes that rise sharply on arrival
  • No business name, address or license (where licensing is required)
  • Pressure to replace a working lock rather than rekey it
  • Refusal to give a written invoice

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a locksmith install a lock I bought on Amazon?

Most will, if it is a known brand and complete. Some charge a little more and will not warranty the hardware.

Is it cheaper to buy my own lock?

Often the hardware is cheaper, but labor may be higher. Compare the total of both options before deciding.

Can a locksmith rekey a new lock to match my old key?

Usually yes, for a per-cylinder fee, as long as the keyways are compatible.

Do handymen install locks?

Yes, for simple swaps into existing holes. Use a locksmith for rekeying, security upgrades and complex locks.

What should I check before buying a lock for a locksmith to install?

Backset, door thickness, hole sizes, lock function, ANSI grade and that all parts are in the box.
