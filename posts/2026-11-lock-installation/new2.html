A secure front door lock is two pieces of hardware working together: a handleset or keyed knob/lever for everyday latching, and a separate deadbolt about 6" above it for real security. Buy them as a matching, keyed-alike set, install the latch first and the deadbolt second, then reinforce both strike plates with 3" screws. This guide covers choosing the hardware and installing it on a standard pre-drilled entry door in about 90 minutes.

Choosing Your Front Door Hardware

Handleset vs. Knob or Lever Combo

  • Handleset: a tall exterior grip with a thumb latch, paired with an interior knob or lever and usually a matching deadbolt. It looks upscale and suits wide or tall entry doors.
  • Knob or lever combo pack: a keyed entry knob or lever plus a matching deadbolt in one box. Simpler to install and usually less expensive.
  • Keypad or smart deadbolt: replaces the keyed deadbolt for keyless entry while you keep a standard handle below.

Security Grade

Choose ANSI/BHMA Grade 2 or better for both pieces. Grade 1 deadbolts resist the most force and are worth it on doors that are hidden from the street. For more on grades and finishes, see our door knobs and handles guide.

Measure Before You Buy

  • Backset: usually 2-3/4" on entry doors (2-3/8" on some).
  • Door thickness: usually 1-3/4" for exterior doors.
  • Hole spacing: handlesets need a specific center-to-center distance between the handle and deadbolt holes, often 5-1/2". Measure yours before buying a handleset.

Tools You Need

  • Phillips screwdriver and drill/driver
  • Tape measure
  • 1" chisel and hammer (for strike adjustments)
  • 3" screws for both strike plates
  • Lock installation kit, only if your door is not pre-drilled

How to Install a Front Door Lock: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Remove the Old Hardware

Unscrew the interior side of the old knob and deadbolt, pull both halves out, then remove the latch and bolt from the door edge. Keep the old parts until the new set is working.

Step 2: Check the Holes

Confirm the face holes are 2-1/8" and the edge holes are 1". If you are fitting a deadbolt to a door that has only a knob hole, follow our guide on how to install a deadbolt on a wooden door to drill the second hole.

Step 3: Install the Latch

Set the latch to the correct backset, insert it with the angled side facing the direction the door closes, and screw the faceplate in flush.

Step 4: Install the Deadbolt Bolt

Insert the deadbolt with "UP" facing up and screw its faceplate in. Leave the bolt retracted.

Step 5: Mount the Exterior Hardware

For a handleset, fasten the exterior grip first: the lower post usually screws through the door from inside, and the upper part is held by the deadbolt cylinder. For a combo pack, slide the keyed knob or lever through the latch from outside.

Step 6: Mount the Interior Hardware

Fit the interior knob or lever and the deadbolt thumb turn, insert the through-bolts and tighten evenly. Do not overtighten: it is the most common reason new hardware feels stiff.

Step 7: Install and Reinforce the Strike Plates

Mark where the latch and bolt meet the jamb, deepen the bolt hole to about 1-1/4" if needed, and fit both strike plates flush. Replace the short screws with 3" screws into the framing. Consider a heavy-duty strike box or a door reinforcement plate for extra protection against kick-ins.

Step 8: Test Everything

With the door open, test the key, thumb turn and handle. Then close the door and test again. The deadbolt should extend its full 1" without you pushing or lifting the door.

Common Mistakes

  • Relying on the knob lock alone. A spring latch can be slipped with a card. The deadbolt is what secures the door.
  • Keeping short strike screws. They only grip the thin jamb, not the stud.
  • Buying a handleset without checking hole spacing. Mismatched spacing means extra drilling and visible old holes.
  • Mixing brands without checking keys. You will end up carrying two keys unless you rekey.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best lock for a front door?

A Grade 1 or Grade 2 single-cylinder deadbolt paired with a keyed entry handle or handleset, installed with a reinforced strike and 3" screws.

Can I install a front door lock myself?

Yes. On a pre-drilled door, a combo pack takes about an hour and a handleset about 90 minutes with basic tools.

Do I need both a knob lock and a deadbolt?

Yes, for security. The knob or lever latches the door; the deadbolt is the real lock.

How much does front door lock installation cost?

Hardware runs about $60 to $300. A locksmith or handyman typically charges $75 to $200 for installation, more if new holes are needed.

Can I make my new lock use my existing key?

Often, yes. Locksmiths can rekey most cylinders, and some brands let you rekey at home with a tool and your current key.

The Bottom Line

A front door lock installation is really two jobs done together: fit a quality keyed handle for everyday use and a Grade 2 or better deadbolt for security, then reinforce both strikes with 3" screws. Measure your backset and hole spacing before buying, and the install is a comfortable DIY afternoon.
