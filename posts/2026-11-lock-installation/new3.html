To install a lock on a metal door, use a bi-metal hole saw with cutting oil at slow speed, drill from both sides so you do not dent the steel skin, and support the hollow core so the through-bolts do not crush the door. Most residential steel entry doors are already prepped with standard holes, so you can often skip drilling entirely. This guide covers both situations, plus commercial hollow metal doors.

Tap the door with your knuckle and look at the edge. A painted wood strip along the edge means a residential steel door. Solid metal all around usually means commercial hollow metal.

Tools and Materials

  • Bi-metal hole saw, 2-1/8" (with arbor and pilot bit)
  • 1" bi-metal hole saw or step bit for the edge (metal edges)
  • Variable-speed drill (corded is best)
  • Cutting oil
  • Center punch and hammer
  • Metal file or deburring tool
  • Painter's tape and the lock's paper template
  • Self-tapping sheet metal screws if the door edge is steel
  • Safety glasses and gloves

Option 1: Door Is Already Prepped (Most Common)

Most steel entry doors come from the factory with a 2-1/8" knob hole, often a deadbolt hole, and 1" edge holes. In that case:

  1. Measure the backset (usually 2-3/8" or 2-3/4") and door thickness (usually 1-3/4").
  2. Buy matching hardware, Grade 2 or better for exterior doors.
  3. Install the latch or bolt and secure the faceplate. On wood edges, use the supplied screws; on steel edges, the door may have pre-tapped holes.
  4. Fit the exterior and interior sides and tighten the through-bolts until snug.
  5. Install the strike in the frame and test.

The full process is the same as our front door lock installation guide.

Option 2: Drilling a New Lock Hole in a Steel Door

Step 1: Mark Carefully

Tape the template to the door at the right height (about 36" for a knob, 6" higher for a deadbolt). Center-punch the hole centers so the drill does not skate on the steel.

Step 2: Drill the Face Hole at Low Speed

Apply cutting oil and drill the 2-1/8" hole with steady, light pressure at low speed (around 300 to 500 RPM). Let the saw cut; pushing hard overheats the teeth and dents the skin. Stop when the pilot bit pokes through, then finish from the other side.

Step 3: Drill the Edge Hole

On a wood-edged door, use a 1" spade bit. On a steel edge, use a 1" bi-metal hole saw or step bit with cutting oil.

Step 4: Deburr the Edges

File away sharp burrs inside both holes so they do not cut your hands or bind the lock.

Step 5: Support the Core

Foam-core doors can crush when you tighten through-bolts. Many lock kits include a reinforcer sleeve; use it. If not, a steel door reinforcer plate or wrap-around plate spreads the load and adds security.

Step 6: Install the Lock

Fit the latch or bolt, attach the faceplate (self-tapping screws on steel edges), install both sides and tighten the through-bolts until snug, then test.

Installing a Strike in a Metal Frame

Hollow metal frames usually come with a strike prep already cut. If not, drill a series of holes with a step bit, file the opening to shape, and fasten the strike with machine or self-tapping screws. For extra kick resistance, fill the frame behind the strike with grout or install a strike box, as long as your local code and the frame manufacturer allow it.

Commercial Hollow Metal Doors

Commercial doors are often prepped for a specific lock type: cylindrical (bored), mortise or exit device. Fit the lock to the existing prep. Cutting new preps into fire-rated doors can void the fire label. Use only listed hardware and have a qualified door technician do any field modifications.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a wood hole saw. It dulls instantly and tears the steel.
  • Drilling too fast without oil. This overheats the saw and leaves jagged edges.
  • Crushing the foam core. Overtightened through-bolts bow the skin and bind the lock.
  • Modifying a fire-rated door. Field cuts can void the rating.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you drill a lock hole in a steel door?

Yes. Use a bi-metal hole saw, cutting oil and low speed, and drill from both sides to keep the skins clean.

Are metal door locks different from wood door locks?

Residential steel doors use the same standard locks as wood doors. Commercial hollow metal and aluminum doors may need specific lock types or narrow-backset hardware.

What screws do I use on a metal door?

Use the supplied screws on wood edges. On steel edges and frames, use self-tapping sheet metal or machine screws that match the pre-tapped holes.

How do I keep a steel door from denting when I drill it?

Center-punch first, use light pressure and cutting oil, and finish each hole from the opposite side.

Can I add a deadbolt to a steel door?

Yes. Follow the same steps as our deadbolt installation guide, swapping in a bi-metal hole saw and cutting oil for the steel faces.

The Bottom Line

Most metal doors are easier than they look: residential steel doors are usually pre-drilled and take standard locks. When you do need to drill, the right bi-metal hole saw, cutting oil, slow speed and core support are what make the job clean and secure.
