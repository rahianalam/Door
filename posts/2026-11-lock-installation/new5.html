Most keypad door locks install in 20 to 30 minutes on a door with standard holes: set the latch backset, run the exterior keypad's cable through the bore, connect it to the interior unit, mount both halves, insert batteries, then program your codes. The steps below work for most brands, including Schlage, Kwikset, Yale and Ultraloq. Always keep the manufacturer's manual handy for brand-specific details.

Types of Keypad Locks

  • Keypad deadbolt: replaces your deadbolt. The most popular choice for front doors.
  • Keypad lever or knob: replaces the handle. Common on garage entry and side doors.
  • Smart keypad lock: adds Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Z-Wave or Matter for app control, guest codes and alerts.
  • Mechanical keypad lock: push-button, no batteries. Great for sheds, gates and utility rooms.

Tools You Need

  • Phillips screwdriver (avoid power drivers on the final tightening)
  • Tape measure
  • Batteries (usually 4 AA; check the box)
  • Chisel, only if the new bolt faceplate is a different size

How to Install a Keypad Door Lock: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Remove the Old Lock

Unscrew the interior thumb turn, pull both halves out, and remove the old bolt from the door edge. Keep the old lock until the new one works.

Step 2: Set the Backset and Install the Bolt

Adjust the new bolt to your backset (usually by rotating or sliding the faceplate). Insert it with "UP" or "TOP" facing up and screw it in. Leave the bolt retracted.

Step 3: Install the Exterior Keypad

Route the keypad's ribbon cable under the bolt (not through it) and pass the tailpiece through the bolt mechanism. Keep the keypad level against the door.

Step 4: Connect and Mount the Interior Unit

Fit the mounting plate, feed the cable through its opening and connect it to the interior unit's socket. Tuck any excess cable so it is not pinched. Line up the tailpiece with the thumb turn and attach the interior unit with its screws.

Step 5: Tighten by Hand

Snug the screws with a hand screwdriver. Overtightening is the most common reason keypad locks jam or drain batteries quickly, because the motor has to fight the friction.

Step 6: Check the Strike

With the door open, extend the bolt by hand. Then close the door and lock it. The bolt should move freely into the strike. If it rubs, adjust the strike or deepen the hole to about 1-1/4". Use 3" screws in the strike for security.

Step 7: Insert Batteries and Run Setup

Many motorized locks run a handing calibration when batteries are first inserted: the bolt moves to learn which way your door locks. Keep the door open and let it finish.

Step 8: Program Your Codes

Use the programming code from the back of the keypad or the manual (or the brand's app) to create a user code. Test it several times with the door open, then closed.

Brand Notes

  • Schlage keypad deadbolts: lock from outside by pressing the Schlage logo button. See how to lock a Schlage lock from outside.

Common Mistakes

  • Pinching the cable. A damaged ribbon cable means a dead keypad.
  • Installing the bolt upside down. The motor calibrates incorrectly and may not lock.
  • Skipping the strike check. A rubbing bolt causes jams and drains batteries.
  • Losing the programming code. Photograph the code label before mounting the lock.
  • No backup entry. Keep the physical key somewhere accessible outside the house, in case the batteries die.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I install a keypad lock myself?

Yes. On a door with standard holes, most keypad deadbolts install in 20 to 30 minutes with just a screwdriver.

Do keypad locks fit any door?

They fit most doors with a 2-1/8" bore, 1" edge hole and 1-3/8" to 1-3/4" thickness. Very thick doors need an extension kit.

What happens when the batteries die?

Most keypad locks warn you weeks ahead with a low-battery light. If they die, use the backup key, or on many models hold a 9V battery to terminals under the keypad to power it temporarily.

How long do keypad lock batteries last?

Typically 6 to 12 months, depending on use, Wi-Fi features and how smoothly the bolt moves.

Are keypad locks secure?

A Grade 2 or Grade 1 keypad deadbolt installed with a reinforced strike is as secure as a quality keyed deadbolt, and removes the risk of hidden spare keys.

The Bottom Line

Installing a keypad door lock is one of the easiest security upgrades you can make. Match the backset, route the cable carefully, tighten by hand, make sure the bolt slides freely into the strike, and save your programming code. You will have keyless entry working in about half an hour.
