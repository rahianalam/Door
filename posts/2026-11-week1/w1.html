On a uPVC or composite door, you double lock it by closing the door, lifting the handle fully upward, then turning the key (or thumb turn) until it stops. Lifting the handle throws the hooks and rollers along the door edge; turning the key locks them in place. If the handle won't lift or the key won't turn, the door is out of alignment, not broken. In the US, "double locking" usually means something different, which is covered below.

How to Double Lock a uPVC or Composite Door

These doors use a multipoint lock: one long mechanism inside the door edge that drives several hooks, rollers or bolts into the frame at the top, middle and bottom at the same time. The handle engages them; the key locks them.

  1. Close the door fully. Push it gently against the seals. It should sit flush with the frame.
  2. Lift the handle all the way up. You'll feel the hooks and rollers shoot into the frame. On the inside, lift the inside handle.
  3. Turn the key (outside) or thumb turn (inside) about a full turn until it stops. This locks the gearbox so the handle can't be pushed down.
  4. Test it: push the handle down. If it doesn't move, the door is double locked.

To unlock, turn the key back first, then push the handle down.

Why it matters

If you close a uPVC door without lifting the handle and turning the key, only the latch holds it. The door can often be opened by pushing a card or tool past the latch, and many home insurance policies require doors to be locked with the key when the house is empty. Locking with the key also takes pressure off the handle, which helps the gearbox last longer.

If the handle is hard to lift or the key won't turn

This is the most common uPVC complaint, and it is almost always alignment, not a faulty lock:

  • Door has dropped: the hooks catch on the keeps instead of sliding in. Adjust the hinges (most uPVC hinges have height and lateral adjustment screws under a cover cap).
  • Keeps are too tight: the frame keeps can be adjusted or packed out slightly so the door closes more easily.
  • Seasonal swelling: doors often get stiff in hot weather and loosen in winter.

Don't force the handle. Forcing it is the main way gearboxes break. If gentle hinge adjustment doesn't help, call a uPVC door specialist.

Timber Doors with a Night Latch and Mortice Lock

Many older UK wooden front doors have two locks: a night latch (rim lock, often a Yale-type cylinder) and a mortice deadlock lower down. Double locking means:

  1. Close the door so the night latch catches.
  2. Lock the mortice deadlock with its key.
  3. If your night latch has a deadlocking function, lock that too (see below).

For insurance, the mortice lock usually needs to meet BS 3621 (look for the kitemark on the faceplate).

US Doors: Knob Lock + Deadbolt

In North America, people searching "how to double lock a door" usually want to know whether to use the knob lock, the deadbolt, or both. The answer: always use the deadbolt. The knob or lever lock is a spring latch that can be slipped; the deadbolt is a solid bolt that can't be pushed back. Locking both is fine, but the deadbolt does the real work.

  • From outside: lock the deadbolt with your key (and the knob, if keyed).
  • From inside: turn the deadbolt thumb turn so the bolt extends fully.
  • Make sure the bolt goes all the way into the frame. If it only locks when you push the door, the strike needs adjusting. See how to install a strike plate.

A double-cylinder deadbolt (key needed on both sides) is a different thing. It isn't required for "double locking" and is restricted by many codes on exit doors because it slows escape in a fire.

Night Latches with a Deadlocking Feature

Some night latches can be deadlocked so the latch can't be slipped or opened from inside without a key:

  • Key-deadlocking latches: turn the key the opposite way after closing, or turn the inside snib with its own key.
  • Auto-deadlocking latches: a small plunger next to the latch deadlocks it automatically when the door closes.

Common Mistakes

  • Closing a uPVC door and walking away without lifting the handle and turning the key.
  • Forcing a stiff handle instead of adjusting the hinges or keeps.
  • Leaving the key in the inside of the lock, where it can be reached through a letterbox or broken glass.
  • Relying on a US knob lock without the deadbolt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to lift the handle to lock a uPVC door?

Yes. On a multipoint lock, the key can't turn until the handle has been lifted to engage the hooks and rollers.

Can a uPVC door be locked from inside without a key?

Yes, if it has a thumb turn on the inside. Lift the handle, then turn the thumb turn.

Why does my uPVC door lock from inside but not outside?

Usually a worn cylinder cam or a misaligned door. See door locks from inside but not outside.

Should I replace my uPVC cylinder?

If it is an older, unbranded euro cylinder, upgrading to an anti-snap cylinder is one of the best security improvements. See how to replace a door lock cylinder.

The Bottom Line

For uPVC and composite doors: close, lift the handle, turn the key. For US doors: always throw the deadbolt. If any step feels stiff, fix the alignment before something breaks.
