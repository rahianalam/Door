Installing an interior door frame means fitting three pieces (two side jambs and a head jamb) into the rough opening, then shimming them plumb, square and level before nailing. Expect 2 to 3 hours for your first frame. This guide covers a frame built from a jamb kit or flat jamb stock. If you're replacing a whole door, a pre-hung unit is faster; see our complete door installation guide.

Materials and Tools

  • Jamb kit or jamb stock (finger-jointed pine, MDF or hardwood), matched to wall thickness
  • Composite or tapered wood shims (one bundle)
  • 2-1/2" finish nails or trim screws; 3" screws for the hinge side
  • 4' or 6' level, tape measure, combination square
  • Miter saw or handsaw, hammer or finish nailer, drill/driver
  • Pencil and utility knife

Cut List (Example: 32" × 80" Door)

PieceQuantityLength
Side jambs2Door height + 1/8" head gap + head jamb dado depth + floor clearance (typically ~81-1/4" to 81-1/2"; measure your floor)
Head jamb1Door width + 1/4" (1/8" gap each side) + 2 × dado depth

Jamb kits are pre-cut long and dadoed. Trim the side jambs so the head jamb sits at the right height above the finished floor.

Step 1: Check the Rough Opening (15 min)

Measure the width at the top, middle and bottom, and the height on both sides. Check that the floor is level across the opening and the jack studs are plumb. Fix anything more than 1/2" out now; it's far easier than fighting it later.

Step 2: Assemble the Frame (20 min)

Lay the jambs on a flat surface. Fit the head jamb into the dadoes at the top of the side jambs and fasten it with 2 to 3 screws or nails through each side jamb. Check the inside width equals the door width plus 1/4".

Step 3: Set the Frame in the Opening (10 min)

Tip the frame into the opening and center it. The jamb edges should sit flush with the drywall on both faces of the wall. Tack nothing yet.

Step 4: Shim and Fix the Hinge Side First (30 min)

  1. Place shim pairs (one from each side, so they stay parallel) at the top, behind each hinge position, and at the bottom.
  2. Adjust until the hinge jamb is plumb in both directions and straight. Check with the level on the face and the edge.
  3. Nail through the jamb and shims into the jack stud. At each hinge location, drive a 3" screw through the jamb into the stud to carry the door's weight.

Step 5: Set the Head Jamb Level (10 min)

Shim above the head jamb at each end if needed so it is level, then fasten.

Step 6: Set the Latch Side (20 min)

Shim the latch jamb at the top, middle (behind the strike location) and bottom. Keep the gap to the hinge jamb equal from top to bottom. Use the door itself or a spacer stick cut to door width + 1/4".

Step 7: Check for Square and Twist (10 min)

  • Diagonals: measure corner to corner both ways; they should match within 1/8".
  • Twist: stretch two strings diagonally across the opening from corner to corner. They should just touch where they cross. If they gap or press hard, one jamb is twisted. Adjust the shims.

Step 8: Trim Shims and Prepare for the Door (15 min)

Score the protruding shims with a utility knife and snap them off flush. Then hang the door, mortise hinges if needed, and fit the latch and strike. For hinge mortises, see which router bit to use for door hinges.
