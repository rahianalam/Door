Most US doorbell transformers output 10V, 16V or 24V AC. Older mechanical chimes usually run on 10V to 16V. Most wired video doorbells need 16V to 24V AC with enough power (VA) behind it, and 16V / 30VA is the most common safe choice. Doorbells are always AC, never DC. Match the voltage to your chime, and the VA to your doorbell.

Always check your doorbell's and chime's specifications. Requirements vary by brand and model.

Volts vs. VA: The Two Numbers on the Label

  • Volts (V): the electrical "pressure". Your chime is designed for a specific range. Too low and it won't ring or the video doorbell reboots; too high and the chime plunger hits harder, wearing it out faster.
  • VA (volt-amps): how much power the transformer can supply. Video doorbells draw power constantly for Wi-Fi and the camera, so they need more VA than a simple push button. A higher VA rating than you need does no harm.

Rule of thumb: pick the voltage your chime can handle, and the highest VA your doorbell recommends.

How to Find Your Transformer's Voltage

  1. Find the transformer. Common spots: attached to a junction box near the electrical panel, in the basement, attic, garage, closet, or behind the chime cover.
  2. Read the label. It lists the output voltage and VA, such as "16V 10VA".
  3. No label? Measure it. Set a multimeter to AC volts (V~). Touch the probes to the two low-voltage screw terminals on the transformer, or the "Front" and "Trans" terminals at the chime. A reading slightly above the rated voltage is normal with no load.

Signs Your Transformer Is Too Weak

  • Video doorbell keeps rebooting or going offline
  • Night vision or live view drops out
  • Chime buzzes or hums instead of ringing
  • App reports "low power"

Safety: Who Can Replace It?

The low-voltage side (chime and button wiring) is safe to work on. The transformer's primary side connects to 120V household wiring. Turn off the breaker before touching it, and if you're not comfortable working with mains wiring, hire an electrician. See do you need an electrician to install a doorbell.

Quick FAQs

Is a doorbell transformer AC or DC?

AC. Standard doorbell transformers step 120V AC down to low-voltage AC (10V, 16V or 24V).

Can I use a 24V transformer with a 16V chime?

Not recommended. The chime may work, but it will strike harder and can wear out or buzz. Use 24V only if the chime is rated for it.

Does a higher VA damage my doorbell?

No. VA is the transformer's maximum capacity; the doorbell only draws what it needs.
