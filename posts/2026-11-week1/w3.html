Most keypad deadbolts lock from outside with one press of a single button: the brand logo or a padlock icon on the keypad. No code is needed to lock, only to unlock. Some models can also lock themselves after a set time. Find your brand below.

Button labels and auto-lock options vary between models and firmware. If your keypad doesn't respond as described, check the manual for your exact model number.

Schlage

Close the door and press the Schlage logo at the bottom of the keypad. The bolt extends and the light flashes. If it doesn't lock, see our Schlage not locking from outside troubleshooting guide. Full model-by-model steps: how to lock a Schlage door from outside.

Kwikset SmartCode

  1. Close the door fully.
  2. Press the lock button on the exterior keypad. On touchscreen models, touch the screen first to wake it.
  3. Listen for the motor and check that the bolt has fully extended.

Many SmartCode models can be programmed to auto-lock after about 30 seconds. On models with a key cylinder, you can always lock with the key too.

Yale Assure and Real Living

  1. Close the door.
  2. Touch the keypad to wake it (the Yale logo or lock icon lights up).
  3. Press the lock icon or logo. The bolt throws.

Yale's "Auto Re-lock" setting relocks the door automatically after a delay, and can be turned on in the lock's menu or app.

Smart Locks with App Control

For Wi-Fi and Bluetooth smart locks, you can usually lock in three ways: a button on the keypad, the app, or automatically using auto-lock or geofencing. Turn on auto-lock with a delay long enough that you aren't locked out when stepping outside briefly. If the lock relocks unexpectedly, the auto-lock delay is too short.

If you use auto-lock, always carry the code and keep a working key hidden securely, or rely on a model with a door-position sensor.

Why the Keypad Won't Lock from Outside

  • Weak batteries: the motor has enough power to light the keypad but not to throw the bolt. Replace all batteries.
  • Bolt rubbing on the strike: it locks with the door open but not closed. Adjust the strike.
  • Wrong handing: the lock tries to turn the wrong way. Recalibrate.
  • Keypad lockout: after several wrong codes, many keypads ignore input for a short time.

If the lock is locking itself unexpectedly, see why a door locks automatically when closed.
