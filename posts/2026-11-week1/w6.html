Short answer: choose solid core doors for bedrooms, bathrooms and home offices, where sound and feel matter; hollow core is fine for closets and low-use rooms; and choose solid wood when you want a natural wood look or a stained finish. Solid core gives most of the benefits of solid wood at a lower price and with better stability. Here's how the three compare.

What's Inside Each Door

  • Hollow core: two thin skins (hardboard or MDF) over a cardboard honeycomb grid, with a solid wood frame around the edges and a solid block where the handle goes.
  • Solid core: the same type of skins over a dense core of particleboard, MDF or composite wood. It's solid all the way through, but not natural wood inside.
  • Solid wood: made entirely from timber, usually as panels in a frame of stiles and rails.

How to Tell Which Door You Have

  • Knock test: hollow core sounds hollow and drummy in the middle; solid core and solid wood sound dull and solid.
  • Weight: lift the door slightly by the handle. Hollow core moves easily.
  • Edge check: look at the top or bottom edge. Solid wood shows real end grain; solid core shows particleboard or MDF; hollow core shows a thin wood frame.

Things to Know Before Upgrading

  • Hinges: heavier solid doors need three good hinges, ideally with a 3" screw in each top hinge into the stud.
  • Locks: hollow core doors only have solid blocking at the handle location, so you can't add a deadbolt elsewhere. See how to install a lock on an interior door.
  • Trimming: don't cut more than about an inch off a hollow core door or you'll expose the honeycomb.
  • Soundproofing needs more than the door: gaps under and around the door let sound through, so add weatherstripping and a door sweep for the full benefit.

Bottom Line

For most homes, solid core doors are the best value upgrade in the rooms you live in, and hollow core is fine where nobody notices. Choose solid wood when the look matters most. For exterior doors and other materials, see our door materials guide.
